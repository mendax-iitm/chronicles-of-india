Pratihara Zenith & Imperial Cholas

प्रतिहार उत्कर्ष और चोल उदय
c. 920 CE · Span: 850 CE to 999 CE
Mihira Bhoja makes Kannauj the undisputed capital of northern India. In the south, Vijayalaya revives the Cholas at Thanjavur, while Amoghavarsha I fosters Kannada literature at Manyakheta.

Territorial Polities & Realms

  • Gurjara-Pratihara Empire: Mihira Bhoja and Mahendrapala I rule an empire from Gujarat to Bihar; patron of poet Rajashekhara.
  • Rashtrakuta Empire: Amoghavarsha I reigns 64 peaceful years at Manyakheta, authoring Kavirajamarga.
  • Pala Empire: Devapala and Mahipala I preserve Buddhist monastic learning and bronze sculpture art.
  • Chola Kingdom: Vijayalaya captures Thanjavur; Parantaka I conquers Madurai and gold-plates Chidambaram.
  • Pandyas & Cheras: Rajasimha II flees to Lanka; Kulasekhara Alwar revives Chera realm at Mahodayapuram.
  • Hindu Shahis: Jayapala rules from Peshawar and Lahore against the Ghaznavid rise.
  • Eastern Ganga (Odisha): Consolidating rule in Kalinga and coastal Utkala.
  • Lanka: Facing repeated maritime Chola invasions under Parantaka I.

Key Rulers in this Era

  • Amoghavarsha I Nrupatunga (Rashtrakuta) · 814 CE–878 CE
  • Mihira Bhoja I (Gurjara-Pratihara) · 836 CE–885 CE
  • Rajaraja I the Great (Imperial Chola) · 985 CE–1014 CE
